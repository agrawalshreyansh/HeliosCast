== GRID MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS AND BEST PRACTICES ==

--- SECTION 1: SOLAR VARIABILITY MANAGEMENT ---
Protocol SV-01: High Irradiance Variability Response
When solar irradiance drops more than 30% within a 1-hour window, grid operators must:
1. Activate spinning reserves within 5 minutes.
2. Issue ramp-up signal to peaker plants or gas turbines.
3. Reduce export to the grid and prioritize local battery discharge.
4. Notify demand-response participants to reduce load.

Protocol SV-02: Cloud Cover Thresholds
- Cloud cover < 20%: Normal operation. Solar output expected near capacity.
- Cloud cover 20–50%: Moderate variability. Pre-charge batteries to 80% state-of-charge (SoC).
- Cloud cover > 50%: High variability. Activate full battery backup. Engage grid import reserves.

--- SECTION 2: BATTERY STORAGE BEST PRACTICES ---
Protocol BS-01: Optimal Battery Charging Windows
Charge batteries during peak solar hours (typically 09:00–14:00 local time) when irradiance exceeds 600 W/m².
Avoid charging when SoC > 90% to preserve battery lifespan.
Target a minimum 40% SoC at sunset to handle overnight baseload.

Protocol BS-02: Discharge Priority Rules
Discharge batteries when:
- Grid frequency drops below 49.8 Hz (indicating under-supply).
- Spot market electricity prices exceed $0.15/kWh.
- Solar generation drops below 20% of rated capacity during daytime hours.

Protocol BS-03: Battery Health Management
Limit depth of discharge (DoD) to 80% of total capacity.
Avoid rapid charge/discharge cycles exceeding 2C rate.
Schedule maintenance discharge cycles monthly to calibrate State-of-Charge sensors.

--- SECTION 3: GRID EXPORT AND IMPORT DECISIONS ---
Protocol GE-01: Feed-in Tariff Optimization
When solar generation exceeds local demand by more than 15%:
1. Export surplus to the grid only when spot price > $0.08/kWh.
2. If price < $0.08/kWh, store surplus in batteries.
3. Log all export events with timestamps and watt-hours for settlement.

Protocol GI-01: Grid Import Triggers
Import from grid when:
- Battery SoC < 20% and solar generation < 50 W/m².
- Forecasted night demand exceeds stored energy by more than 10%.
- Import price is below the cost of battery wear ($0.03/cycle equivalent).

--- SECTION 4: DEMAND RESPONSE STRATEGIES ---
Protocol DR-01: Peak Shaving
Use solar + battery combination to flatten peak demand between 17:00–21:00.
Pre-cool or pre-heat facilities during solar hours to shift HVAC loads.
Alert industrial consumers 1 hour before expected peak to reschedule heavy loads.

Protocol DR-02: Load Shedding Hierarchy
In case of severe solar shortfall (> 60% generation loss):
Priority 1 – Maintain critical loads (hospitals, data centers, water treatment).
Priority 2 – Reduce commercial HVAC by 15%.
Priority 3 – Suspend non-critical industrial processes.
Priority 4 – Rolling residential load curtailment (5-minute blocks).

--- SECTION 5: FORECASTING-BASED PRE-DISPATCH ---
Protocol FP-01: Day-Ahead Scheduling
Use ML-based solar forecasts (e.g., Linear Regression, LSTM) to pre-schedule:
- Battery charge/discharge cycles.
- Spinning reserve activation.
- Demand response notifications.
Submit day-ahead schedule to grid operator by 10:00 AM local time.

Protocol FP-02: Intra-Day Adjustment
Re-run forecast model every 4 hours.
If forecast deviates > 20% from actual, trigger automatic reserve dispatch.
Use ensemble forecasting during monsoon or winter cloud-cover seasons.

--- SECTION 6: SAFETY AND ISLANDING PROTECTION ---
Protocol IS-01: Anti-Islanding Measures
All distributed solar systems must comply with IEEE 1547 anti-islanding standards.
Automatic disconnection within 2 seconds when grid voltage deviates ±10% from nominal.
Synchronization check before reconnecting to the main grid after an outage.

Protocol IS-02: Fault Ride-Through
Solar inverters must maintain grid connection during voltage dips of up to 15% for 0.5 seconds.
During prolonged faults (>2 seconds), controlled shutdown with ramp-down at 10% per second.

--- SECTION 7: REGULATORY AND REPORTING ---
Protocol RR-01: Metering and Settlement
Install bi-directional smart meters for all solar prosumers.
Report net energy import/export to distribution company (DISCOM) monthly.
Maintain 1-year data retention for regulatory audits.

Protocol RR-02: Renewable Portfolio Standards
Solar assets must contribute to RPS targets as mandated by local energy regulator.
Document all curtailment events with reasons for regulatory submissions.
Annual performance report must include capacity factor, availability factor, and PR (Performance Ratio).
